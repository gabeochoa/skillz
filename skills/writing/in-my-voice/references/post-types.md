# Post types

Four types cover almost every announcement someone writes for a team. They differ in section
order, not in tone, and picking the wrong one is the most visible way a draft reads as
generated.

Read the type off the request, then confirm it against what the author's own posts of that
type look like. The section orders below are the common shape, and the profile overrides them
wherever the author consistently does something else.

| Type | Signals in the request | Section order |
|---|---|---|
| Launch | launched, shipped, live, announce, released | summary line, context, what shipped, impact, next steps, resources, credit, notify |
| FYI or share | sharing, new guide, heads up, FYI, available now | summary line, why it matters, what is in it, resources, notify |
| Feedback | issues, feedback, problems, broken, does not work | summary line, grouped issues each with evidence, impact |
| Update | update, progress, status, results, where we are | summary line, what changed, measures, next steps, notify |

## What each type owes the reader

Launch: what exists now that did not exist before, and who it is for. An impact section with
no measure in it is filler, so cut the section rather than write "significant improvement".

FYI: why the reader should spend attention on this. The most common failure is a post that
describes a resource without saying who needs it.

Feedback: evidence per issue, one link or reproduction each. Group by theme, not by the order
they were found. Impact goes last and says what it costs, not how frustrating it is. This is
the type where an assertive voice reads as blame if the evidence is missing, so the evidence
carries it.

Update: the delta since the last update, and the measures. An update that restates the plan
without a delta is not an update.

## Credit and notification

Most people have a fixed closing pattern: a credit line, then who to notify. Copy theirs
exactly, including whether it is a hashtag, a heading, or a plain sentence.

Name what each person did. "Thanks to everyone involved" credits nobody and is the single
most common tell that a draft was not written by the person whose name is on it.
