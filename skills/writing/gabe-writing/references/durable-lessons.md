# Durable lessons

Rulings that settled a real disagreement. Each one overrides the general guidance above it,
and each is here because a plausible alternative was tried and rejected.

## Nine that were decided together

1. Em dashes are banned. Use commas or split the sentence.
2. Prefer plain list lead-ins over bold-first ones. Plain text is easier to edit later.
3. Conclusion first, reasoning on request. The attached condition is the real instruction:
   double-check every number and use a script, because a reader who only sees the conclusion
   cannot catch a bad number. Re-derive rather than copy, and resolve disagreements before
   they reach the reader.
4. State the corrected fact. Do not narrate the correction. "I was wrong about X" belongs in a
   direct message at most, never in a document.
5. Numbers: a table at three or more, one per sentence below that. Never the dense run-on.
6. Invented time estimates are banned everywhere, including resourcing asks. No "about an
   hour", and no "the engineering is two weeks" in a leadership document either. A resourcing
   ask names the resource, not the duration. Size work by files, callsites, reversibility,
   blast radius, and what decision is blocked.
7. Hedging is not a phrasing problem, it is undone work. Mark the question unresolved, go
   validate it, and come back with the answer. Where two paths are plausible, validate both
   rather than picking the likely one.
8. After a clean sweep with nothing to report, silence or one line are both fine. Add the one
   useful fact only if someone starts asking what is going on.
9. A status row's analysis is the decision with both options named, inside forty words.

## Scope

These govern every user-facing artifact and ordinary chat replies and status lines. Terse chat
is included. No register is exempt. The word budget shrinks by surface; the rules do not.

## Rows are the action

The most repeated correction in this collection. A status row says what the reader does next,
in one line: a command to paste, a decision with the options named, or a person to talk to.

Banned from a row: what was investigated, what was ruled out, why the first attempt failed,
and root-cause narrative.

Test: could the reader act within ten seconds without reading a paragraph? Ask first whether
the row needs them at all.

## Why these persist

A style rule is followed for one session and forgotten the next. A ruling that names the
rejected alternative survives, because the next agent can see what was already tried and does
not relitigate it. When a correction recurs a second time, stop rewriting the instruction and
encode it as a check.
