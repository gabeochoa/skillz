# Precedence, and the standing exceptions

Two rules disagree often enough that the order needs to be written down.

## Order

1. An explicit format the reader asked for in the moment. It beats everything below, no
   argument.
2. The voice profile plus the AI-tells list. These two are the gate every artifact passes.
3. The supporting references, in this order among themselves: local exceptions, plain-language
   rules, controlled-language rules for technical writing.

Anything published under a person's own name follows their voice profile even where a style
rule disagrees.

## Standing exceptions

These override the general checklist. Each one exists because following the rule literally
produced a worse artifact.

1. Domain vocabulary is not metaphor slop. If a word already appears in the code, the ticket,
   the doc, or the meeting, it is the right word even when a style list calls it jargon. Test:
   does the term exist in the material, or did the writer invent it?
2. Emoji are banned in prose and required as the leading status marker on a status row. The
   row is scanned, not read, and the marker is what makes it scannable.
3. A low reading level is not the goal. Professional register is fine. Invented words and
   airless rhythm are the actual defect.
4. Parentheses stay. Some AI-tell lists ban them alongside em dashes. Parenthetical asides are
   a common feature of real personal writing. Ban the em dashes and keep the parentheses.

## Where controlled language applies

Apply it to specs, runbooks, change summaries, test plans, and notes a stranger reads without
context.

Do not apply it to writing in a person's own voice, to short status rows, or to posts. Orwell's
sixth rule covers the general case: break any of these sooner than say anything outright
barbarous.

## Keeping precedence honest

Precedence rots when the underlying files move or disappear. Two habits keep it usable:

- Name one file as canonical for each rule. When a source is lost, promote the surviving
  summary to canonical and say so in the file, rather than leaving a pointer to nothing.
- Date each ruling. A rule with no date cannot be superseded, so it never gets corrected.
