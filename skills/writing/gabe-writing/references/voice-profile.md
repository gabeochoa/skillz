# Voice profile: how to compile one, and how to apply it

A voice profile is the empirical half of this standard. The checklist in `SKILL.md` says what
not to write. The profile says what this particular person actually sounds like when they
write well, which is the only thing that stops sanitized prose from reading like everyone
else's sanitized prose.

## Compiling one

Gather the person's own archive: long-form posts, short chat messages, and review comments.
A few hundred posts and a couple hundred messages is enough to see stable patterns; a dozen
is not.

Extract, per register:

1. Sentence length distribution, and whether it varies or flatlines.
2. Capitalization and punctuation habits, including the ones a style guide would call errors.
3. Filler and hedge words they genuinely use, as opposed to the ones models add.
4. How they open. Most people have two or three habitual openers.
5. How they close. Many strong writers simply stop, with no closing line at all.
6. Structural habits: numbered lists, tables, parenthetical asides, one thought per line.

## Registers

Keep at least three, because a rule that fits a long post ruins a one-line reply.

| Register | Typical use | Budget |
|---|---|---|
| Long form | a post, a design doc, an explanation | as long as the facts need |
| Working note | a report, a change summary, a status update | a screen at most |
| Reply | chat, a comment, an acknowledgement | one to three lines |

The word budget shrinks by register. The banned-construction list does not.

## Applying it

- The profile beats a general style rule wherever the two disagree, for anything published
  under the person's own name.
- The profile never beats an explicit format the reader asked for in the moment.
- Do not launder a personal register into neutral corporate prose. Lowercase starts, dropped
  apostrophes, and "yea" are the profile working as intended.
- Do not imitate a register the person only uses with a specific audience. A profile compiled
  from public posts does not license that voice in a formal document.

## The failure this prevents

Without a profile, an agent writes competent, airless prose that no colleague would mistake
for the person. The recorded correction that produced this file was blunt: writing "more
professionally" is fine, writing "like an alien" is not. The problem is invented words and
an airless register, not vocabulary size.
